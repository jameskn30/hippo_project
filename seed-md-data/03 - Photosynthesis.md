# Photosynthesis

## Overview

Photosynthesis converts **light energy into chemical energy** stored in carbohydrates. Plants, algae, and some bacteria carry out this process. Their stored energy supports much of the food web, including organisms that cannot make their own food from sunlight.

## Inputs and outputs

| Inputs | Outputs |
| --- | --- |
| Light energy | Chemical energy stored in carbohydrates |
| Carbon dioxide | Carbohydrates |
| Water | Oxygen in oxygen-producing photosynthesis |

In plants, photosynthesis generally takes place in **chloroplasts**, especially in leaves. Chlorophyll helps absorb light.

## A simplified equation

```text
6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2
```

This equation summarizes an overall process with many intermediate reactions. It does not mean that a leaf makes glucose in one single step.

## Why it matters

- Photosynthetic organisms supply food directly or indirectly to many other organisms.
- Oxygen-producing photosynthesis releases oxygen into the environment.
- Light becomes stored chemical energy that organisms can use later.

## Remember

Plants transform energy; they do not create it.
