# Electricity Basics

## Overview

Electricity involves electric charge and its movement. The electricity used in homes and workplaces is an **energy carrier**: it transfers energy that can become light, heat, or motion.

It is also a **secondary energy source**. Other energy sources are converted into electrical energy before it reaches many of the devices we use.

## Where electricity comes from

Examples of primary energy sources used to generate electricity include:

- Natural gas and coal
- Nuclear energy
- Wind
- Sunlight

The generating source matters. Electricity itself is not automatically renewable simply because an appliance uses it.

## Everyday uses

| Device | Useful result |
| --- | --- |
| Light | Illumination |
| Heater | Heat |
| Electric motor | Movement |
| Computer | Information processing |

## AC and DC

**Direct current**, or DC, and **alternating current**, or AC, are different ways of supplying electric current. The development of AC systems helped make transmission over long distances more practical.

## Remember

An electrical device is part of an energy chain: energy source, generation, delivery, and use.
