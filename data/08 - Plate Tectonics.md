# Plate Tectonics

## Overview

Earth's rigid outer layer is divided into moving **tectonic plates**. Their interactions help explain many earthquakes, volcanoes, ocean ridges, and mountain ranges.

Plate motion is slow on a human timescale, but its effects accumulate over millions of years. Movement and deformation are especially important near plate boundaries.

## Common boundary types

| Type | Motion | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divergent | Plates move apart | Mid-Atlantic Ridge |
| Convergent | Plates move toward each other | Ocean trenches and continental collisions |
| Transform | Plates slide past each other | San Andreas Fault |

At a divergent boundary, rising material can create new oceanic crust. At some convergent boundaries, one plate descends beneath another; this is called **subduction**. When continents collide, their relatively buoyant crust can build mountain ranges.

## How motion is measured

- Patterns in seafloor rocks reveal earlier movement.
- Matching rock formations across a fault can reveal displacement.
- Repeated satellite measurements track changes in position.

## Remember

Some boundaries are broad, complicated zones rather than neat lines.
